\section{Envelope constructions}\label{sec:constructions}

Each of the three envelope construction methods follows the same two stage calibration procedure: the shape-discovery set $S_1$ is 
used to determine the geometric shape of the envelope, then the size scaling set $S_2$ is used to determine the scaling factor 
$\hat t$. Each construction assigns a scalar score, $\tau(v,m)$, to a score vector $v$ with observed set $m$. A candidate class is 
then included in the prediction set when $\tau(v,m)\le\hat t$, where $\hat t$ is calculated on $S_2$ as in Section~\ref{sec:setup}. 
For both the collapsed and radial methods, we define $\tau(v)=\inf\{t>0: v\in t\cdot E\}$ using an envelope $E$ which is star shaped 
with respect to the origin and give a closed form expression for it. The strip method works in the same way, except that the 
single scaling factor $\hat t$ multiplies the limits of the staircase rather than dilating the whole region, so $\tau$ is defined 
directly as a maximum of ratios.

We first fix a class $h$ and drop the index $h$. $K$ denotes the number of coordinates that remain after removing the coordinates 
that are missing for every sample of $S_1$. For a vector $v\in[0,\infty)^K$ and observed set $m\subseteq\{1,\dots,K\}$, we write 
$v_k$, $k\in m$, for just its observed entries. We use $\varepsilon=10^{-12}$ to prevent division by zero, and $Q_p(\cdot)$ denotes 
the empirical $p$-quantile.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\paragraph{Collapsed envelope.} The basic envelope construction simply collapses the $K$-dimensional score vector to a scalar score 
by taking the mean over the observed coordinates, and calibrates a single quantile on $S_1$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapsed}
\bar v=\frac{1}{|m|}\sum_{j\in m}v_j,\qquad \tilde q=Q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\{\bar v_i\}_{i\in S_1}\bigr),\qquad
\tau(v,m)=\frac{\bar v}{\tilde q},\qquad
E=\{v:\bar v\le\tilde q\}.
\end{equation}

This method discards the geometric information that the $K$ coordinates provide. Two vectors with the same mean are treated 
identically whatever the distribution of their coordinates is, so a single coordinate with strong evidence (a very small $v_k$) is 
averaged together with all the others. The accepted region is a halfspace in the observed coordinates. It serves as a mean score baseline 
for the multidimensional constructions below. After calibration on $S_2$, a vector is accepted when $\bar v/\tilde q\le\hat t$, 
which is the same as $\bar v\le\hat t\,\tilde q$. Here $\hat t$ is the $k$-th smallest value of $\bar v_i/\tilde q$ over $S_2$, 
so $\hat t\,\tilde q$ is just the $k$-th smallest value of $\bar v_i$ over $S_2$. 
% This means that $\tilde q$ does not change the prediction set, and for this method $S_1$ is only 
% used to decide which coordinates are kept.



%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\paragraph{Radial envelope.} Let $u_1,\dots,u_M$ be unit vectors in the nonnegative orthant ($M=250$ by default), obtained by 
normalizing the absolute value of Gaussian vectors with a fixed random seed. For $s\in S_1$ with observed set $m_s$, let $\|s\|$ 
be the Euclidean norm of its observed entries and $\hat s$ its direction (zero on missing entries). For each $r$, let 
$N_r=\{s\in S_1:\langle \hat s,u_r\rangle\ge\cos\theta\}$ with $\theta=30^\circ$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radii}
\rho_r=Q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\{\|s\|:s\in N_r\}\bigr)
\end{equation}
when $|N_r|\ge n_{\min}=\max\{10,\lfloor 0.2\,|S_1|\rfloor\}$. If $0<|N_r|<n_{\min}$, $\rho_r$ is the convex combination of the 
local quantile and the marginal quantile $Q_{1-\alpha}(\{\|s\|:s\in S_1\})$ with weight $|N_r|/n_{\min}$ on the local one, and if 
$N_r=\emptyset$ it is the marginal quantile. A smoothed radius in direction $a$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth}
R(a)=\sum_{r=1}^{M}w_r(a)\,\rho_r,\qquad
w_r(a)=\frac{\exp(\kappa\langle a,u_r\rangle)}{\sum_{r'}\exp(\kappa\langle a,u_{r'}\rangle)},
\end{equation}
with $\kappa=8$. Evaluating it at the anchors $a_t\in\{u_1,\dots,u_M,e_1,\dots,e_d\}$ (the $e_j$ are the coordinate axes) gives 
boundary points $b_t=R(a_t)\,a_t$, and the score is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial}
A^{\mathrm{rad}}(v,m)=\min_{t}\;\max_{j\in m}\;\frac{v_j}{b_{tj}+\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
Since $A^{\mathrm{rad}}(v,m)\le q$ means that $v_j\le q\,b_{tj}$ for all observed $j$ for some $t$, the accepted region is a union 
of boxes with a corner at $q\,b_t$, restricted to the observed coordinates.



\paragraph{Radial envelope.} The radial envelope method retains the directional structure of the scores by calibrating a boundary radius as a function of direction on the unit sphere, restricted to the positive orthant since all nonconformity scores are nonnegative. We draw $M$ directions $\{u_r\}_{r=1}^M$ uniformly over the orthant by sampling $g_r\sim|\mathcal N(0,I_K)|$ coordinate-wise (with a fixed random seed) and normalizing, $u_r=g_r/\|g_r\|$. For each $v_i\in S_1$, over the observed coordinates only, we compute its magnitude $\|v_i\|$ and its unit direction $d_i=v_i/\|v_i\|$ (with zeros on the missing coordinates). For each direction $u_r$, we collect the $n_r$ points of $S_1$ within an angular sector of half-width $\delta=30^\circ$, that is, those with $d_i\cdot u_r\ge\cos\delta$. The directional radius $\tilde q_r$ is then calibrated as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radii}
\tilde q_r=\begin{cases}
Q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\{\|v_i\|:d_i\cdot u_r\ge\cos\delta\}\bigr) & \text{if } n_r\ge n_{\min},\\[1ex]
w_r\,Q_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\{\|v_i\|:d_i\cdot u_r\ge\cos\delta\}\bigr)+(1-w_r)\,\tilde q_{\mathrm{marg}} & \text{if } 0<n_r<n_{\min},\\[1ex]
\tilde q_{\mathrm{marg}} & \text{if } n_r=0,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $n_{\min}=\max\{10,\lfloor 0.2\,|S_1|\rfloor\}$ is a minimum support threshold, $w_r=n_r/n_{\min}$, and $\tilde q_{\mathrm{marg}}=Q_{1-\alpha}(\{\|v_i\|\}_{i\in S_1})$ is the marginal fallback radius. This blend avoids a hard cutoff between sufficient and insufficient local support. To get a smooth boundary, we compute the radius in an arbitrary direction $d$ as a weighted average of all $M$ calibrated radii,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth}
R(d)=\sum_{r=1}^{M}\omega_r(d)\,\tilde q_r,\qquad
\omega_r(d)=\frac{\exp(\kappa\,d\cdot u_r)}{\sum_{r'=1}^{M}\exp(\kappa\,d\cdot u_{r'})},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa>0$ controls the angular smoothness (a larger $\kappa$ gets closer to nearest-direction assignment). We evaluate $R$ at the anchor directions $a_t\in\{u_1,\dots,u_M,e_1,\dots,e_K\}$, where the $e_j$ are the coordinate axes, which gives the boundary points $b_t=R(a_t)\,a_t$. The envelope $E$ is the union of the boxes $\{v:0\le v\le b_t\}$ over all $t$, and the scaling function has the closed form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial}
\tau(v,m)=\min_{t}\;\max_{j\in m}\;\frac{v_j}{b_{tj}+\varepsilon},
\end{equation}
which is $\inf\{t>0:v\in t\cdot E\}$ computed on the observed coordinates. We use $M=250$ and $\kappa=8$ by default.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\paragraph{Strip envelope.} For every coordinate $j$, the interval from the smallest to the largest observed $S_1$ value is cut into $B=8$ bins of equal width, and $b_j(x)\in\{1,\dots,B\}$ is the bin of $x$ (values outside the interval go to the end bins). Let $\alpha'=\alpha/d$. For each ordered pair $j\ne i$ and each bin $b$, the limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limits}
L_{ji,b}=Q_{1-\alpha'}\bigl(\{s_i:s\in S_1,\ s_j,s_i\text{ observed},\ b_j(s_j)=b\}\bigr)
\end{equation}
is the quantile of coordinate $i$ among $S_1$ samples whose coordinate $j$ falls in bin $b$. Bins with fewer than $3$ samples are blended with the marginal quantile of coordinate $i$ in the same way as in the radial case. The limits are then made nonincreasing in the bin index,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monotone}
\tilde L_{ji,b}=\max_{b'\ge b}L_{ji,b'} ,
\end{equation}
and the score is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strip}
A^{\mathrm{strip}}(v,m)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\max_{\substack{j,i\in m\\ j\ne i}}\ \dfrac{v_i}{\tilde L_{ji,b_j(v_j)}+\varepsilon}, & |m|\ge 2,\\[3ex]
\dfrac{v_j}{q_j+\varepsilon}, & m=\{j\},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $q_j=Q_{1-\alpha'}(\{s_j:s\in S_1\ \text{observed}\})$. The level $\alpha/d$ is a Bonferroni-type choice for the $d$ coordinates. It affects the shape of the score only: the coverage of the final set is set by $\widehat q$. Because the bin is chosen from the unscaled value $v_j$, the thresholded regions are score sublevel sets and not dilations of one fixed region.

\paragraph{Properties.} Table~\ref{tab:comparison} lists the main differences between the methods. The three scores share one structural property.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:monotone}
Fix an observed set $m$ and let $v,v'$ have the same observed set with $v'_j\le v_j$ for all $j\in m$. Then $A(v',m)\le A(v,m)$ for each of the three scores. Hence every region $\{A\le\widehat q\}$ is downward closed: if a label is accepted, it stays accepted when its scores improve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \eqref{eq:collapsed} this is clear because the mean is nondecreasing in each coordinate. In \eqref{eq:radial} each ratio $v_j/(b_{tj}+\varepsilon)$ is nondecreasing in $v_j$, and a maximum and a minimum of nondecreasing functions is nondecreasing. For \eqref{eq:strip}, $v'_j\le v_j$ gives $b_j(v'_j)\le b_j(v_j)$, so $\tilde L_{ji,b_j(v'_j)}\ge\tilde L_{ji,b_j(v_j)}$ by \eqref{eq:monotone}. Together with $v'_i\le v_i$ every ratio in \eqref{eq:strip} gets smaller. The case $|m|=1$ is again a ratio nondecreasing in $v_j$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is not used for the coverage statement. For fixed $S_1$ (and fixed directions $u_r$, which are drawn independently of $S_2$ and of the new observation) each score is a fixed function of $(v,m)$, so the rank argument of Section~\ref{sec:setup} applies to all three in the same way. Monotonicity is only a sanity property of the learned shapes.
